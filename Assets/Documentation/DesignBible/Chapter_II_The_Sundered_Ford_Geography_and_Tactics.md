### THE GEOGRAPHY OF THE FORD

The Sundered Ford is the sole theater of the Phase II Vertical Slice for THE WARBORN MARCH. It is a broken river crossing mapped precisely to the browser's Strike reckoning. Access to the Ford is completely free. It is here that the player first tests the exact, 1:1 physical SKU from the armory—striking true against the environment and the enemy. The ground itself is designed to manifest the numerical friction between the Grogens (the Deep Dig) and the Daminari (the Legion).

### THE EAST BANK: THE LEGION'S STRENGTH

The Daminari claim the eastern shore. The terrain here is elevated, composed of solid, unyielding stone shelves. This high ground naturally favors the Legion's disciplined shield walls and high Guard stats. The rock cannot be entrenched; it demands that a line be held through sheer formation and Vigour. Sight lines from the eastern ridges are dominant and unbroken, commanding a clear view of the river's center, allowing Daminari commanders to identify exactly where an assault will hit the shore.

### THE WEST BANK: THE DEEP DIG

The Grogens deploy on the western shore. Unlike the east, this bank is a landscape of churned mud, loose shale, and deep, diggable soil. The terrain bends to the Grogens' philosophy. Sight lines here are intentionally obstructed by natural mounds and rapidly carved dugouts. The ground shields the Deep Dig from the Legion's high-ground gaze, allowing them to mass their Might unseen before surging toward the water. It is a place for sudden ambushes and subterranean resilience.

### THE CROSSING: CHOKEPOINTS AND ICE

Between the banks lies the Ford—a span of rushing, knee-deep water choked with the debris of old wars. The water strips momentum. It is mathematically designed to be a breaking point; no unit can hold a line in the water indefinitely without their Vigour draining to zero. You either hold the shore, or you commit entirely to the crossing.

Here, Ice is the sole live magic. A player expending their Magical meter can instantly freeze sections of the rushing water. This transforms a brutal, muddy chokepoint into a smooth, high-speed vector for a flanking maneuver, or creates a sudden, temporary wall of ice to shore up a crumbling defense before the river claims it.

### LIGHT, WEATHER, AND STILLNESS

The Ford is locked in a bitter, overcast chill. The lighting is diffused and slate-grey, emphasizing the grim reality of the March. 

Strict engine boundaries govern the atmosphere: there is no strobing red when blades meet, and the shimmer of the rushing river pulses at a gentle 1.5 Hz, safely below the 3 Hz ceiling. For players who engage the stillness setting, the rushing water renders as a stylized, beautiful static texture, and the ambient snowfall pauses completely. The tactical read of the field remains pristine, ensuring accessibility without compromising the cold, desolate atmosphere.

### THE FIRST NINETY SECONDS

In the first ninety seconds of this free trial, the geometry of the Ford asks the player three immediate tactical questions:

1. **The Question of the Water:** Do I push my real-SKU blades into the river immediately to force a crossing, or do I dig into the western mud and let the Legion blunt their Might against my trenches?
2. **The Question of the Frost:** Do I spend my Magical meter early to freeze the river and carve a new flanking sight line, or save the Ice to stall the Daminari heavy infantry when they step off the eastern rock?
3. **The Question of the Dragon:** I have one hireable Dragon Contract. Do I sign it to shatter the unyielding eastern high ground to blind the Legion, or deploy it over the water to guarantee a safe crossing for my vanguard?
