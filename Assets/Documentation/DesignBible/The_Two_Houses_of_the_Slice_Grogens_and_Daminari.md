### The Principle of the Hand

Within ninety seconds, a stranger must feel the exact nature of their chosen house through the mouse or touch-screen alone. We use no tutorial pop-ups and no text to explain the martial doctrine of the Grogens or the Daminari. Instead, we use tempo, audio, UI responsiveness, and the visual language of the meters. Because this slice operates within the Return Current, the weapons wielded are the exact SKUs sold in the armoury. A heavy Grogen axe and a swift Daminari spear must not merely look different; they must dictate a completely different rhythm of play.

### The Grogens: The Deep Dig

Commanding the Grogens must feel like swinging a siege engine. They are a house of immense, localized pressure, punished entirely by the passage of time.

**The Mechanic:** The Deep Dig allows one banner to be buried in the diggable western earth of the Sundered Ford before the Muster. It waits, striking from beneath when the ground above is contested.

**The Feel:** The Grogens are defined by slow Vigour and heavy Guard. 
*   **Audio and Visual Weight:** Every strike lands with the auditory crack of a fallen timber. The animation frames emphasize the wind-up and the follow-through. (Any screen-shake must strictly obey the stillness setting, utilizing deep bass thuds rather than visual distortion to convey impact).
*   **The Leash of Vigour:** The Vigour meter creeps upward agonizingly slowly. A player feels immediate tension watching this bar. They learn instantly that they cannot afford a prolonged skirmish. 
*   **The Dig:** When the buried banner strikes from beneath, the earth erupts with heavy rock particles—a sudden, violent release of the tension built by their slow Vigour. They feel invincible in the initial clash, but if the enemy survives the opener, the slow crawl of the Vigour bar induces immediate panic.

### The Daminari: The Legion

Commanding the Daminari must feel like weaving a net. They are a house of rapid, fluid repositioning, entirely reliant on adjacency and completely ruined by isolation.

**The Mechanic:** Adjacent banners share Guard. Alone, their Guard is brittle. Together, they form an unbreakable phalanx.

**The Feel:** The Daminari are defined by rapid Vigour recovery and situational Guard.
*   **Visual Tethers:** When Daminari banners are placed side-by-side, their Guard meters visually lock together, bridged by a subtle, glowing undercurrent (never strobing, pulsing at a calm 1.5 Hz). The player sees the UI physically reinforce itself.
*   **The Fracture:** If the line is broken—either by a Grogen ambush from below or a misplay into the broken river crossing—the visual tether snaps. The audio cue is sharp, like fracturing ice or shattering glass. The Guard meters instantly thin out.
*   **The Rhythm:** Their Vigour meter refills rapidly. The player feels a constant invitation to act, to maneuver, to strike and step back. They feel lethal and untouchable while the line holds, and terrifyingly fragile the second a unit is caught alone.

### The Convergence at the Sundered Ford

The terrain of the Phase II slice inherently teaches the doctrines of the two houses. The broken river crossing forces the Daminari to carefully navigate their formation, naturally seeking the eastern high ground where their line cannot be flanked. The diggable western earth invites the Grogens to bury their banner, setting an anvil against which the Daminari must eventually break.

When Ice magic—the sole live magic of the slice—is deployed, it interacts violently with these established rhythms. A frozen Daminari unit shatters the adjacency tether, instantly compromising the Legion. A frozen Grogen unit halts their already-slow Vigour recovery, turning their patience into a death sentence. By the time the dragon departs on time, the player has mastered the weight of their house simply by trying to survive the Ford.
